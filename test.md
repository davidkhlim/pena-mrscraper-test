# 🎯 ROUND 1: Client Complaint (Core PM)

### 👤 Interviewer:

> A client says: “Your data is wrong and incomplete.” What do you do?

### ✅ Your baseline answer:

> “First, I’d validate the issue by reproducing the exact case—checking the input, timing, and returned data.
>
> Then I’d compare expected vs actual behavior, including factors like source limitations or interpretation differences.
>
> From there, I’d align both the client and engineering team on what’s actually happening before deciding next steps.”

---

### 🔥 Pushback:

> “That sounds standard. What if the client is frustrated and demands an immediate fix?”

### 💪 Strong response:

> “In that case, I’d separate immediate response and root cause.
>
> I’d acknowledge the issue and give a clear short-term update, even if it’s just confirming we’re investigating.
>
> At the same time, I’d continue validating internally so we don’t commit to the wrong fix.
>
> The key is managing expectation while making sure the solution is accurate.”

---

# 🎯 ROUND 2: Upsell / Growth (CEO concern)

### 👤 Interviewer:

> How would you upsell to a client?

### ✅ Baseline:

> “I don’t approach it as selling—I try to understand the client’s workflow and identify gaps. If they repeatedly request more data or manual adjustments, that usually indicates an opportunity to expand the solution in a way that improves their outcome.”

---

### 🔥 Pushback:

> “What if the client says they don’t want to pay more?”

### 💪 Strong response:

> “Then I’d step back and understand why—whether it’s budget, unclear value, or timing.
>
> If the value isn’t clear, I’d reframe it in terms of their outcome—like saving time or improving accuracy.
>
> If it’s still not the right timing, I wouldn’t force it. I’d focus on delivering well first, because strong delivery builds trust, and that usually opens up opportunities later.”

# 🎯 ROUND 3: Prioritization

### 👤 Interviewer:

> You have 5 clients requesting changes at the same time. What do you do?

### ✅ Baseline:

> “I’d prioritize based on impact, urgency, and dependency.
>
> Client-blocking issues or revenue-impacting tasks would come first, followed by high-value improvements.
>
> I’d also communicate clearly to each client on expected timelines to manage expectations.”

---

### 🔥 Pushback:

> “What if all clients say their request is urgent?”

### 💪 Strong response:

> “That’s where I’d align urgency with impact. Not all urgent requests have the same business impact.
>
> I’d prioritize based on actual risk—like blocking operations vs minor improvements—and communicate transparently so clients understand the reasoning.”

---

# 🎯 ROUND 4: Engineer Conflict

### 👤 Interviewer:

> Engineer says “works on my machine,” client says “it’s broken.” Who do you trust?

### ✅ Baseline:

> “I wouldn’t rely on either side immediately—I’d validate the case myself using the same inputs and conditions.”

---

### 🔥 Pushback:

> “What if you can’t reproduce it?”

### 💪 Strong response:

> “Then I’d dig into differences—like environment, timing, or data conditions.
>
> Often the issue is in assumptions, so my role is to make those explicit and align both sides on facts before deciding next steps.”
